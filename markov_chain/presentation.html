1 · Project framing

From Parker's solos to an operator on a finite state space

Research question. Can the syntactic rules of bebop improvisation (voice leading, harmonic gravity, the on-beat / off-beat asymmetry) be recovered as numerical properties of a Markov-chain operator estimated from Parker's solos?

Teaser: top, real Anthropology head; below, one trajectory sampled from the fitted chain. Both are projections: the chain moves through 428 states, and the plot shows only the scale-degree coordinate of each (0 = root … 11 = major 7th), collapsing the chord-quality and beat coordinates. R = rest event, below the dotted rule.

Top: the pickup and first two bars of Anthropology as Parker played them. Bottom: the opening 16 events of the same synthetic trajectory plotted on the left, entering on the downbeat of bar 2 over the identical changes.

Each event the chain emits is a full state: a scale degree (or a rest), a chord quality, and a beat position. It carries no register and no duration, so octave is supplied by nearest-neighbour voice leading and the rhythm laid out as straight eighths.

3 · State space

State = (scale degree, chord quality, beat position)

Three discrete coordinates per event (note or rest):

  • Scale degree d ∈ {0, …, 11} ∪ {R}: for a note, semitones above the active chord root d = (pcnote − pcchord-root) mod 12; for a rest, the sentinel R.
  • Chord quality q ∈ Q = {maj7, min7, dom7, m7♭5, dim7}: the active chord at event onset (rests inherit it).
  • Beat position b ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, &1, &2, &3, &4}: an integer beat if |offset mod 1| < 0.05 quarter-notes, else offbeat, labelled by the beat it follows: "&4" is anywhere between beat 4 and the barline.

Why this state space

  • Scale degree abstracts over transpositions: the same line over F7 and over B♭7 collapses to one state.
  • Chord quality respects the functional context: a "♭7" sounds like the dominant 7th, the half-diminished 7th, or the dim7 depending on quality.
  • Beat position separates structural notes (downbeats) from passing material (see bebop's passing notes).

|S| ≤ 13 × 5 × 8 = 520; observed: 428.

The encoding collapses 23,143 events onto 428 labels: about 54 visits per state, and every tune revisits most of its states several times over. That recurrence is what makes the transition probabilities estimable at all.

4 · The operator, concretely

P̂ is a table: 428 rows, one question each

Every row asks one question: Parker is on this state, where does he go next? Walk the 23,143 events in order; each time state i is followed by state j, add 1 to cell (i, j), then divide each row by its own total. No modelling, just a tally. The hat marks an estimate: P̂ is what 50 solos say about the unobservable true P.

Probabilities live within a row: each row sums to 1 across all 428 columns.

5 · The walk on a graph

Eight nodes, so the walk is something you can look at

P̂ is a 428-node directed weighted graph: nodes are states, edge i → j carries weight P̂ij. Here is the induced subgraph on the eight states with the most stationary mass, which between them carry 9.7 % of the walk's time.

Every one of the eight is a dom7 state, and seven of the eight sit offbeat. The strongest single edge is (5, dom7, &3) → (rest, dom7, ♩4) at 11 %: run the bebop scale, then breathe.

6 · The assumption

The Markov property: is the current state enough?

Pr(Xt+1 | X0, …, Xt = si)  =  Pr(Xt+1 | Xt = si)

The next state depends only on where the line is, not on how it got there. Everything before the current event is discarded.

This is the licence for the previous slide. If history mattered, Parker could not be summarised as a table of "from → to": you would need one row per phrase, and no corpus could fill it.

It is an assumption, not a fact

Take (5, dom7, &4), which occurs 346 times with a known predecessor, and split its continuations by what came before:

arrived frommost likely continuation
(6, dom7, ♩4)  n = 63(1, dom7, ♩1)   22 %
(3, dom7, ♩4)  n = 45(♭7, dom7, &4)   40 %

Same current state, different futures. Approached from the 3rd, the line keeps descending to the ♭7 four times out of ten; approached from the 6th, that continuation barely appears. The property is false in the strict sense, and we know the direction of the error: descending lines carry momentum a first-order chain cannot see.

8 · Section 2 · Stationary distribution

π solved as the left eigenvector at λ = 1

Perron-Frobenius (smoothed irreducible aperiodic case)

π P = π,   Σs π(s) = 1,   π > 0

→ π exists and is unique. Computed by diagonalizing PT and selecting the eigenvector at the eigenvalue closest to 1, then ℓ¹-normalizing.

The chain recovers its own marginal essentially perfectly (r = 0.9999): the stationary distribution of the fitted operator is the corpus's own state-occupation frequency, which is a consistency check on the whole estimation.

9 · Section 10 · Evolving the distribution

Put all the probability on one state and let it run

Section 2 found π algebraically, as the left eigenvector at λ = 1. Here it is found the other way, by iteration:

μ0 = ei ,    μt+1 = μt P̂

Start with certainty about the note, and watch the certainty dissolve.

Starting on the busiest state, (5, dom7, &4):

  • after 2 steps the total-variation distance to π is already below 0.5;
  • after 21 steps it is below 0.01;
  • at t = 40 the largest disagreement with π is 8.7 × 10−6.

The decay tracks |λ2|t almost exactly, which is the mixing time of Section 3 measured rather than asserted.

10 · Section 3 · Spectral analysis · HEADLINE 1

The spectrum contains a metric cycle

Perron-Frobenius puts λ1 = 1 with the stationary distribution as its left eigenvector. The second eigenvalue governs how fast anything else dies away:

τmix ≈ 1 / (1 − |λ2|)

λ20.596 + 0.555 i
|λ2|0.8143
spectral gap0.1857
τmix5.4 steps

λ2 is complex, and that is the result

A real λ2 would mean the slow mode simply decays. A complex one means it rotates: the chain has an oscillation it returns to. The period is

2π / arg(λ2) = 2π / 0.750 = 8.4 events

One bar of eighth notes is exactly 8 events. The metre is sitting in the spectrum, and nothing put it there: the operator was estimated from counts that know nothing about bar lines.

A corollary: a reversible chain has real eigenvalues, so a complex λ2 proves this walk is not reversible. Time runs one way in it, which is what voice leading requires.

All 428 eigenvalues in the complex plane. The conjugate pair off the real axis is the metric cycle; everything inside the small disc has decayed within a few events.

11 · Section 4 · Hitting times

Musical "gravity" recovered as expected resolution time

For an absorbing target R, the expected hitting time h(i) from i ∉ R satisfies the linear system

h(i) = 1 + Σj ∉ R P̂ij h(j),   h(j) = 0 for j ∈ R

Equivalently, with Q the sub-block of P̂ on S \ R:

h = (I − Q)−1 · 1

Laplace smoothing keeps I − Q invertible.

Operatormedian hmin hshortest sink
P̂4.091.84(7, min7, &1)

Headline

  • The fastest resolution is the leading tone over a minor chord, 1.84 events; then the ♭9 over a dominant at 2.10 and the ♭2 over a major chord at 2.13.
  • The shortest hitting times are exactly the canonical bebop approach tones: chromatic neighbors, leading tones, 2nd degrees. All eight fastest-resolving states sit offbeat: approach tones live in the swing pickup, not on downbeats.
  • Classical pedagogy ("dissonances resolve quickly") emerges as a quantitative property of the operator.

12 · Section 5 · Voice-leading recovery · HEADLINE 2

The V7→I half-step resolution as a single matrix cell

Joint transition probability under the stationary distribution:

Pr(Xt = i, Xt+1 = j)  =  πi · P̂ij

Ranking the cells of π ⊗ P̂ reveals Parker's most-trafficked moves: which transitions actually carry the traffic in the corpus.

The canonical V7 → I cell

In tonal-harmonic theory, the ♭7 of a dominant resolves down a half-step to the major-3rd of the tonic. With the v3 encoding the canonical resolution maps to (♭7, dom7, &4) → (3, maj7, ♩1): the model now names the pickup position itself, rather than lumping all offbeat events together.

source → targetjointconditional
(♭7, dom7, ♩4) → (3, maj7, ♩1)0.000 %0.05 %
(♭7, dom7, &3) → (3, maj7, ♩1)0.000 %0.09 %
(♭7, dom7, &4) → (3, maj7, ♩1)0.015 %10.71 %
(♭7, dom7, &4) → (3, maj7, ♩2)0.000 %0.01 %

When Parker is on ♭7-of-V7 on the pickup &4 just before a chord change, the very next note is the M3-of-I on beat 1 with conditional probability 10.71 %: the textbook half-step resolution, recovered as one matrix entry. The neighbouring cells are dead: the same ♭7 one beat earlier, or resolving to beat 2 instead, both sit under 0.1 %. The resolution is specific to one source position and one target beat.

13 · Section 9 · Generative validation

The chain reproduces its own stationary distribution under simulation

Sample M = 1,000 trajectories of length T = 100 from P̂, starting at uniform initial states (100,000 total samples).

By the ergodic theorem, empirical occupation frequencies should converge to π:

fsim(s) := (# visits to s) / (M · T)  →  π(s)

0.9984
Pearson r(fsim, π)
0.023
TV(fsim, π)

What this tells us

  • The chain is a coherent generative model, not just a descriptive matrix: synthetic Parker lines drawn from P̂ spend the right fraction of their time in each state.
  • Scope of the claim. What converges is the marginal, how much time is spent in each state, not phrase shape. Sampled lines range more widely than Parker's actual contour (see the teaser on slide 2): local melodic shape is an order-2 property, and Section 7 shows held-out likelihood cannot support order-2 at 23k events.

14 · PageRank

PageRank on the graph: which states is the walk most often at?

PageRank is the stationary distribution of a damped walk: with probability d = 0.85 follow an edge of P̂, otherwise teleport uniformly.

r = d · P̂T r  +  (1 − d) / n · 1

The damping is what makes it defined on any graph, including one with dangling nodes. On this operator the smoothing already guarantees irreducibility, so PageRank and π agree closely; the interest is in which states come out on top, and in how the different centralities disagree.

measuretop statetop-10 offbeat
PageRank(5, dom7, &4)8 / 10
in-degree(5, dom7, &4)7 / 10
out-degree(5, dom7, &4)7 / 10
betweenness(rest, dom7, ♩3)1 / 10

The two measures disagree, and the disagreement is the finding. PageRank and degree rank offbeat dominant-chord tones highest: that is where the walk spends its time. Betweenness ranks rests on strong beats highest: that is what the walk has to pass through. Phrase boundaries are the bottlenecks of the graph.

428 nodes, 5,421 directed edges. Betweenness computed with edge weight −log P̂ij, so a "short path" is a probable one.

Top 10 by PageRank, damping 0.85.

  1. V7 → I voice leading appears as a single matrix cell with conditional probability 10.71 %, and the target is specifically beat 1, sharpening the v1 finding from "any on-beat" to "the downbeat of the new chord".
  2. The metre is in the spectrum: λ2 is complex with period 8.4 events, one bar of eighth notes. Nothing put it there; the operator was estimated from counts that know nothing about bar lines.

16 · Further work

What I would do next, and what I tried and set aside

Decomposing the operator

The obvious extension is to split P̂ in two and compare the halves. I built the within-chord / across-chord version and set it aside:

  • The size of the across set is arithmetic, not a finding: 6.23 events per chord gives 1/6.23 = 16 %, exactly the share observed.
  • It is confounded with the downbeat: 59 % of chord changes land on ♩1, and controlling for that the flag moves the chord-tone landing rate by 2 points (63.9 % vs 61.9 %).
  • 76 % of the flag is redundant with the state, which already carries chord quality. Only 889 transitions (3.8 %) are cases where the root moves and the quality does not.

A decomposition earns its place only if it conditions on something not already in the state and not explained by metre.

Splits that would clear that bar

  • Direction (9,505 up / 11,530 down) and step vs leap (11,226 / 8,441): properties of the transition, absent from the state, well balanced. Descending lines already land a chord tone on a strong beat 67 % of the time against 54 % ascending, z = 12.4.
  • Phrase position: first note after a rest versus mid-phrase.

Other threads

  • Duration as a fourth coordinate. Not modelled, for notes or rests; a second-order chain already fails on held-out data.
  • The unobserved cells. 92 of 520 never occur: two structural zeros on well-sampled qualities, the rest scarcity on m7♭5 and dim7.
  • More corpus. 54 visits per state; doubling it is what would make order-2 affordable.
